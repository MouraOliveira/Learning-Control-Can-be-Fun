The control actions

P — proportional: Kp·e
  • Immediate reaction to the current error; speeds up the response.
  • Alone it leaves a steady-state offset (for processes without integration).
  • Too high: overshoot, oscillation, instability; amplifies noise.
I — integral: Ki·∫e dt
  • Removes steady-state error for steps in reference and load disturbance.
  • Adds phase lag: slower, more overshoot, can destabilise.
  • Winds up when the actuator saturates → needs anti-windup.
D — derivative: Kd·de/dt
  • Predicts the error trend, adds damping, permits higher Kp, Ki.
  • Strongly amplifies high-frequency measurement noise.
  • Acting on the error it gives a “derivative kick” at reference steps.
  • Of little help with long dead time.
Filter 1/(Tfs+1)
  • In series with the whole controller: attenuates noise in u and makes D realisable.
  • Too large Tf adds lag in the loop → less damping, eventually instability.
Actuator limits & anti-windup
  • Real actuators saturate: u = sat(v). While saturated the loop is effectively open.
  • Clamping: stop integrating when saturated and the error would push further into the limit.
  • Back-calculation: feed (u−v)/Tt into the integrator so it tracks the limit; smaller Tt = faster tracking.
